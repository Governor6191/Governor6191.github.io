Summary

Benign or malignant from a dermoscopy image, with a calibrated confidence and a measure of how much the ensemble disagrees. Most medical-imaging repos report accuracy and stop. This one reports calibration next to accuracy for every model, because a model that says how sure it is matters more in a clinical setting than one that is only sometimes right and never says so.

The uncertainty method is Deep Ensembles. MC Dropout is included as a cheaper comparison, so the calibration claim is a measured head-to-head rather than a single method asserted on its own. This is the first of two applications on one shared framework, the second being brain tumor segmentation.

1 Why calibration is the point

A model that is only sometimes right and never says so is dangerous in a clinical setting. One that says how sure it is, is useful.

Accuracy alone hides overconfidence. A model can rank cases well and still attach 95 percent confidence to predictions that are right only 80 percent of the time. Calibration measures that gap. The whole framework here is built so that every model reports calibration (expected calibration error, negative log-likelihood, reliability diagrams) next to its accuracy, and so that a single model, a Deep Ensemble, and MC Dropout all run through one comparable path.

2 Data and model

The data is 3000 dermoscopy images pulled from the ISIC Archive, balanced 1500 benign and 1500 malignant, split 70/15/15 into train, validation, and test with a seeded random stratified split so the three sets are drawn from the same distribution. The model is a ResNet-50 backbone (timm, ImageNet-pretrained) with an explicit dropout-and-linear head, trained at 320 pixels. The head dropout is a real module rather than functional dropout, so MC Dropout can switch it on at inference without disturbing batch norm. The Deep Ensemble is five members trained from different seeds.

Held-out test set, 450 images.

The Deep Ensemble is better on every metric, and the calibration gains lead: it cuts NLL by 28 percent and ECE by 37 percent over the single model, while accuracy and AUROC are already near the ceiling on this data. A well-ranked model can still be overconfident, and ensembling is what fixes the confidence, not just the ordering.

MC Dropout barely moves off the single model. That is expected and worth stating plainly: dropout sits only in the classification head here, so the stochastic passes are nearly identical. Full weight diversity across independently trained members does far more than head-only dropout.

6 Honest scope

Read the numbers in context. This is a balanced 3000-image subset drawn from the earlier, well-curated part of the archive, so it is cleaner and easier than the full ISIC collection, and the high absolute AUROC reflects that, not clinical-grade performance. ISIC dermoscopy has known confounds (rulers, ink markings, dark vignetting) that a CNN can latch onto. These numbers are a calibration demonstration, not a diagnostic claim, and the live demo carries a research-tool-not-a-diagnosis disclaimer.
