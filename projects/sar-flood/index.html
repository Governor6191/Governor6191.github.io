Summary

A deep-learning segmentation pipeline that maps flood extent from radar imagery. A U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder reads Sentinel-1 SAR (VV and VH at 10 m) and outputs binary flood masks. Radar sees through cloud and works at night, so it is the data source you can actually use during an active disaster, when storm cover blocks the optical satellites.

The model is trained and benchmarked on Sen1Floods11, then pointed at a real US hurricane it never saw during training. The benchmark number says it generalizes across regions. The Harvey number says what happens on a live disaster, and the gap between the two is real and explained rather than hidden.

1 Why radar

During a hurricane, the optical satellites are blind. The storm is exactly the cloud cover that stops them.

Synthetic-aperture radar does not care about cloud or daylight. It measures backscatter, and calm open water is a near-mirror that reflects the signal away from the sensor, so flooded ground shows up dark. That is the property the model learns. The input is the two Sentinel-1 polarizations, VV and VH, at 10 m resolution. The output is a per-pixel flood mask.

2 Benchmark on Sen1Floods11

Trained and evaluated on the Sen1Floods11 hand-labeled split, the model reaches a water-class IoU of 0.67 and F1 of 0.80 on the held-out test regions. The point of the benchmark is generalization: the test scenes span eleven regions the model is scored on without seeing them during training.

3 Real-disaster validation: Hurricane Harvey

A benchmark number is not the same as a number on a real event. The model is pointed at the Sentinel-1 scene over Houston on August 30 2017, during Hurricane Harvey, and scored against the Copernicus EMS observed flood delineation for the same area, with JRC permanent water removed from both sides so only storm flooding counts.

SettingIoUF1
Sen1Floods11 benchmark, all-hand test, water class0.670.80
Hurricane Harvey 2017, Houston, flood-only0.120.22

Both numbers matter. The gap is real and it is explained: most of the Copernicus reference is flooded vegetation and flooded urban land, which stays bright in radar, and the model only finds water by its dark backscatter. Open-water flood is the part SAR does well. Urban flood from C-band radar alone is not, and the number says so. Honest reporting beats a curated number.

4 Why this target

Why Harvey. Sentinel-1 launched in 2014, so the validation target had to be a post-2014 US disaster with full radar coverage. Hurricane Harvey over Houston in 2017 fits, and the pipeline runs on open data from the benchmark to a real US Gulf Coast disaster, end to end.
